There are seven flags hidden in our API.

We would rather find people by watching them solve things than by reading their CV.

No prior security experience needed. The first three challenges need nothing but a terminal and curiosity. The last one needs you to write actual code — which is the whole point.

curl -i https://asia-south1-collegesapiens.cloudfunctions.net/ctf

The challenges

Flags look like CS{...}. Submit them with POST /submit. From ch04 onwards each flag is minted for you specifically — so a flag pasted into a group chat still points back at whoever earned it, and on ch06 everyone gets a different seed, which makes copying an answer pointless rather than merely dishonourable.

Scope

The difference between a CTF player and an incident is usually just that nobody wrote this part down. So:

Fair game

  • Every endpoint on the CTF service.
  • This site, robots.txt and security.txt.
  • The public source repository.

Off limits

  • The production API, database and storage.
  • Any other student's account or data. Ever.
  • Denial of service, floods, load testing.
  • Social engineering of students, staff or moderators.

If you find something we did not plant

Stop, and do not touch anyone else's data. Email security@collegesapien.com and tell us what you found.

A real, responsibly disclosed vulnerability is worth more to us than every flag on this page combined, and we will say so publicly if you want us to.

The boring but important bit

  • Playing is voluntary. It has nothing to do with your grades, and nothing to do with your account standing.
  • We store which challenges you solved and when, so we can rank solvers and get in touch. That is the entire purpose.
  • The leaderboard is opt-in and shows only the handle you choose — never your email.
  • Ask us at opensource@collegesapien.com and we will erase your CTF record, no questions asked.
